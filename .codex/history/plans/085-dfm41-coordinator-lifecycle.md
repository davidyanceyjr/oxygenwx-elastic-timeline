## Functional contract

**Chosen approach:** Keep readiness, request sequencing, completion and checkpoint ownership inside the existing `CatalogActivationCoordinator`. Add only the narrow state and lifecycle handshake needed by this contract. Do not introduce a second controller, general event bus, polling, or arbitrary delay. Ordinary callers continue using the current activation and selected-time paths.

Implement this as private, purpose-specific coordinator state under its existing synchronization boundary: a current startup-participant identity, the current activation's terminality, per-debug-request completion, and a change notification for capture waiters. These represent distinct facts; neither the UI `loading` flag nor activation completion alone establishes startup readiness. Keep the delegate limited to registering and completing its startup participation, awaiting its own request result, applying fixture selection for that result's generation, and transferring failure to fallback. Preserve the existing ordinary caller interfaces and behavior; do not add a generic lifecycle abstraction.

1. **Startup readiness:** track the startup operation separately from the UI `loading` flag: saved-location restoration is pending during its read even before `loading` changes, and through the resulting initial activation. Do not use `loading` alone as the startup-readiness signal. Empty, invalid and failed restoration settle without inventing a catalog or retry. Alternate cold launch registers synchronously before its asynchronous validation, lookup, activation, and any required fixture selection; failure transfers readiness to the existing fallback with no intermediate settled state. An older participant must not mark a newer operation settled. On supersession, the handler ends its own wait without fallback and readiness follows the current activation to a terminal result. Cancellation propagates and never causes picker, fallback, or false readiness.
2. **Completion:** a narrow coordinator-produced handle resolves success with its selection/generation, failure, or superseded for every debug activation, including replacement during restoration. Fixture time selection occurs only on its own successful generation; the existing generation guard rejects stale selection. Existing ordinary activation callers keep their behavior. Request completion does not itself finish alternate startup participation.
3. **Atomic capture:** a readiness notification alone does not authorize capture. Wait without holding the coordinator lock, then recheck startup readiness and the current activation's terminal state under that same lock and capture before releasing it. If another operation begins, await the appropriate coordinator-owned state change and retry, without polling. Capture selected location and name, last activation request, active selection/interval, pending anchor and complete published state, including exact selected instant and settled empty/error states. The delegate may retain and return the checkpoint but cannot construct, inspect or mutate its internals.
4. **Restoration:** within the coordinator's synchronization boundary invalidate the existing request sequence, cancel in-flight preview and complete superseded waiters, then restore the captured coordinator fields and state directly. If the captured state has an active selection, allocate a fresh monotonic view generation for it; this is the sole exception to exact published-state restoration. Restore its selected instant exactly. A captured state without an active selection needs no new generation. Do not load, fetch, refresh, persist, recompute selection, rewind request/view counters, or expose a parallel activation owner. Late completion and stale fixture callbacks cannot overwrite the restored state.
5. **Compatibility:** no changes to canonical catalog, `CatalogWindow`, codec, schema, namespaces, LIVE/provider/cache behavior or ordinary selected-time presentation. Preserve SG.4 debug cold-launch selection and existing failure/picker behavior.

## Execution guidance for lifecycle races

The approved board and the original owner decisions resolve the intended outcomes; the following narrows how to implement and prove them without adding another owner:

- Associate each startup participant with an identity owned by the coordinator. Only the current participant may settle readiness or transfer it to fallback; a late result from an older saved-location read or alternate launch cannot settle a newer participant. Register alternate startup before launching asynchronous work. Transfer failure to fallback as one synchronized readiness transition, and keep alternate startup pending through its own successful fixture selection. Propagate host cancellation without fallback, picker, or a false ready signal.
- Track whether the current activation is terminal separately from UI `loading`. Preserve the existing sequence and view-generation guards for publication and selected-time callbacks. Supersession ends the displaced handler's own wait without fallback; readiness still follows the current activation to its terminal result.
- Resolve each debug request's coordinator-produced completion exactly once as success, failure, or superseded, including replacement by checkpoint restoration. On success, apply fixture selection only with that result's generation. Preserve ordinary callers and their existing paths.
- Use coordinator-owned change notification rather than delays or polling. Under the lock, check both startup readiness and current activation terminality: if settled, capture before releasing the lock; otherwise obtain the current change notification under that same lock, release the lock, await it, and retry. Signal changes to either condition so a transition between checking and waiting cannot be missed.
- Keep checkpoint representation private to the coordinator and opaque to the delegate. Under the same synchronization boundary, restoration invalidates the current sequence, cancels preview work and settles displaced waiters, then directly restores the captured fields and published state, assigning a fresh monotonic view generation if an active selection is restored. Do not rewind request or view-generation counters, perform I/O, or recompute the selected instant; stale completions and fixture callbacks must not overwrite the restored state.

These are contract-level constraints, not a mandate for a general lifecycle framework. The exact private representation remains an implementation choice. If the existing coordinator cannot satisfy them while preserving ordinary behavior, stop at the owner-resolution boundary in Risks and exclusions.

## Resolved ownership and synchronization details

The approved DFM.4.1 board and the archived DFM.4 restoration contract are the authority for these clarifications. The names below describe responsibilities, not a required API or a claim that the current partial implementation already satisfies them.

- **Startup handoff:** `MainActivity.onCreate` calls the variant delegate before choosing `restoreStartupCatalog()`; the cold-launch delegate therefore registers its participant synchronously before returning `true` and before launching its coroutine. Normal `restoreStartup()` registers its own participant before starting the saved-location read. A fallback must replace the current alternate participant with its saved-location participant in one coordinator-synchronized transition, before the fallback read can suspend; a separate finish followed by an unconditional restore leaves a forbidden ready gap. A stale participant cannot finish, transfer, start fallback over a newer participant, or publish a stale saved-location result. Keep the existing host/delegate seam, changing only what the identity-checked handoff requires.
- **Two independent conditions:** startup participation covers the saved-location read or alternate handler work; the coordinator also tracks whether the *current* activation is terminal. A saved-location read that starts an initial activation transfers to that activation without a capture gap; a successful alternate handler stays pending until its required fixture selection finishes. When a handler's activation is superseded, its own participation can finish on the approved non-canceled path, but capture still waits for the replacement activation. If no activation was started, the activation condition is terminal; an empty, invalid or failed saved read can then settle without a retry. Neither successful request completion nor `loading == false` alone authorizes capture.
- **Completion and cancellation:** request replacement, including restoration, advances the request sequence and resolves the displaced unresolved debug handle as superseded under the same coordinator boundary that establishes the replacement. Success or failure publishes the current request's result and terminal state under that boundary; an already resolved handle keeps its result. Check identity before a late result can publish or finish another operation. The delegate applies SG.4 time only from its own success result and generation; on supersession it skips selection and fallback, preserves the existing picker behavior, and finishes only its own participation. On failure preserve the request error, picker and cold-launch fallback. Host cancellation is rethrown and must not use failure/fallback, picker, or unconditional completion of startup participation; cancellation of a host awaiting a handle must not itself falsely report a successful activation or readiness. A new host/coordinator establishes its own startup participation.
- **Capture and restore linearization:** under the coordinator lock, capture either records all required fields and the published state while both conditions hold, or obtains a notification corresponding to the observed unsettled state. Await outside the lock and recheck; all changes affecting either condition notify waiters. Restore invalidates the current sequence, cancels the in-flight job, settles any displaced completion, restores the saved fields and publishes the restored state as one locked transition. Set the resulting activation condition terminal and notify waiters; do not reset startup participation or counters to captured values. For an active checkpoint increment the existing view-generation counter and change only that generation in the saved published state; for an empty/error checkpoint do not allocate one. The saved selected location name is already part of `CatalogActivationState`; retain it along with the coordinator's separate `selectedLocation`, `lastRequest`, `activeInterval`, and `pendingSelectionAnchor`. The debug consumer gets an opaque handle, not these fields.
- **Deterministic proof boundary:** use controllable saved-read, source and delegate-handshake operations to hold and release each relevant stage; observe capture/completion and picker/fallback calls at those boundaries. In particular, exercise an older startup read finishing after a newer participant registers, fallback handoff with a waiting capture, and an activation beginning after an earlier ready observation but before capture. For restoration, compare the complete saved and restored published states with only the approved generation difference, verify old/new generation selections, and compare I/O spy counts immediately around capture/restore separately from the preview's own I/O. Use a narrow debug lifecycle seam if Android `Intent`/`Activity` dependencies prevent meaningful local proof; `app/build.gradle.kts` currently provides JUnit but no Android lifecycle test framework. The seam must exercise the actual delegate handshake, not a duplicate implementation; if that cannot establish host cancellation and picker/fallback behavior, record the gap and stop as required below.

## Acceptance and focused proof

- Readiness remains pending during a delayed saved-location read with `loading == false`, then resolves after initial activation success or failure and for empty, invalid and failed restoration. A newer activation between a readiness observation and checkpoint capture delays capture until its own terminal state.
- Alternate cold launch remains pending through validation, saved-location lookup, activation and required fixture selection; success, failure/fallback and supersession have the approved outcomes. Fallback transfer has no intermediate ready state; host cancellation neither opens a picker nor triggers fallback or false readiness.
- Debug completion handles terminate on success, failure and supersession, including checkpoint restore; failed/superseded SG.4 requests cannot strand a waiter. Selection is applied only to the successful request's generation.
- Active and settled empty/error checkpoints restore exact state and selected instant, except that a restored active selection has a fresh monotonic view generation. Prove that a callback carrying the captured generation cannot change the restored instant and that a callback carrying the new generation can select normally; a state without an active selection does not allocate a generation. Use controlled deferred operations and fakes/spies to establish the required ordering and that capture/restore perform no provider, source, storage or location persistence I/O, and that canceled/superseded completions cannot overwrite the restored state. Do not rely on timing delays as proof of race behavior. Counters stay monotonic and checkpoint contents remain opaque to the consumer.
- Existing affected coordinator behavior and debug intent/fixture paths remain compatible; release has no debug delegate provider or command behavior added by this slice. Command-entry, ordinary-action ordering, overlay and installed UI acceptance belong to later slices and are not claimed here.
